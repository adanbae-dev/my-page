Search this repository for `d3` and one line comes back.

```
think/branch-strategy.mdx:241
> The diagram is inline SVG. mermaid bundles d3 and dagre and exceeds 500KB
> gzipped,
```

**It appears only as a reason not to use it.** There is no import anywhere in
the code.

And the report that lays out the direction I want to move in puts the
recommended stack like this —
`TypeScript/React → SVG → D3.js → data transformation/SQL → visualisation theory → accessibility/performance`.
D3 sits in the middle of it.

A direction is a claim. This site's rule is that a claim carries its evidence,
so I counted that stack one box at a time.

**SVG, 9 entries.** Every figure on this site is hand-written inline SVG. No
library. They use `currentColor` and `var(--accent)`, so they follow the
site's inversion automatically, and each one is a few hundred bytes.

**Data, 2 entries.** `make/work-calendar` measured real records with
`node:sqlite` and found that what I thought was a task-management tool was
actually a release-management tool — 18 releases against 11 tasks.
`think/statistics-notation` is distributions and notation.

**Accessibility and performance, 3 entries.** The contrast contract in
`think/why-contrast-came-first` is enforced by `pnpm check:contrast`, which
measures the real ratios and fails the build when one drifts.
`make/personal-interface` has its JS budget pinned by a gate.

**D3, zero.** And the three deliverables the report asks for — an analytical
dashboard, one of map/time-series/network, and a reusable chart system — are
all zero too.

## The box I could not count

I did not write zero next to `visualisation theory`. I wrote "uncountable",
and honesty required it.

Drawing nine figures meant choosing, every time, what to show and how. The
figure in `think/the-screen-said-it-was-fine`, written days ago, **draws two
boxes deliberately identical**. That entry's whole argument is that the two
cases are indistinguishable on screen, so the figure had to reproduce that
indistinguishability. The difference appears only on the right — in the
outgoing query — marked in accent.

That is a visual encoding, chosen. But **it was chosen backwards from the
argument, not from theory.** I have never studied normalisation, colour
scales, or the axis choices that mislead, so I have no standard of my own for
judging whether what I picked was right.

Having zero evidence and **having no standard to judge by are different
things.** The first you fix by making something. The second you fix by
learning first.

## The box that hurts most is not D3

The report lists, among the competencies actually checked in hiring,
`accessibility, keyboard navigation, colour safety`.

There is one thing on this site worth calling a visualisation: the WebGL field
in TRACE. One bar per record, height is length, the horizontal axis is time.

And `docs/PRODUCTION.md` says this about it, in my own words:

```
- Keyboard access to the WebGL field. The list is the accessible path;
  roving tabindex over the bars is worth doing and is not done.
```

**On the subject I have written about most, the one visualisation I have
fails.** The contrast contract is pinned by a gate, tap targets were raised to
the WCAG threshold, and the bars still cannot be reached from a keyboard. The
list is an accessible alternative, so it is not inaccessible — but to the
question "can the visualisation be used from a keyboard", this site's answer
is no.

D3 at zero is missing because it was never done. This is **done and then left
out**, which is a different kind of thing.

## Does the reason for rejecting it still hold

The judgement that dropped mermaid in `think/branch-strategy` was this: you
cannot load 500KB gzipped to draw one diagram. That entry's route had 12KB of
JS headroom.

That judgement is still right. **And it does not transfer into a reason to
avoid D3.**

The difference is what the chart is. On this site a figure is **an
illustration serving the prose**. Attach a library to one illustration and it
outweighs the text it illustrates. In an analytical dashboard the chart **is
the product**. The denominator is not the same.

I had put two different things on the same scale and expected the same answer.
Measuring by weight was right; **carrying that measurement across to a
different problem was not.**

So this is a decision to revisit, not a rule to keep. Writing SVG by hand nine
times was not wasted either. To know what `scale`, `axis` and `join` are doing
for you, computing the coordinates yourself first is the faster route.

## One thing already happening

The report's last sentence is this.

> The goal is not someone who draws charts, but someone who understands what
> the data means, chooses the right visual representation, and builds it as a
> high-performance interactive data product.

The middle one — choosing the right representation — is something this site
was already doing. Just never with a chart.

The site's mark is that. `lib/sigil.ts` folds the repository's commit record
into coordinates. One slot per commit, depth is how many lines that commit
changed by hand, the marks reaching past the ring are the phases the history
declared for itself. 64 slots × 3 rings.

**That is data encoded visually.** It simply was not called a chart. And
nobody can paste that mark onto another site, because the data is different.
That is exactly where visualisation parts company with decoration.

## What I did not verify about the report

Half the evidence in this entry is what I counted. The other half is what the
report asserts. The two should not carry equal weight.

The report cites 2026 hiring examples: a Data Visualization Specialist /
Creative Coder at MIT Senseable City Seoul, a UI Data Visualization Engineer
at Adobe, a Data Visualization Engineer 5 at Netflix, a Data Visualization
Engineer at Planet. **I did not open those postings.** They are cited as
grounds that the titles exist, and I am not claiming to have verified what any
of them require.

This is why this site puts gates on its own numbers. Fail to separate what was
checked from what was passed along, and the reader cannot tell which half to
believe.

## About the next six months

The report has a six-month roadmap and I am not copying it here. It belongs to
the report; a second copy is a second thing to keep in step.

What the count above changes is the order.

- **Do not skip month one.** Nine figures are evidence of knowing the medium, not of knowing the theory. That is what "uncountable" meant
- **Pay the keyboard debt first.** The one visualisation I have already fails a standard I already wrote down. That comes before starting three new projects
- **Use D3 first in a product, not in an illustration.** Put it in this site's figures and the judgement I rejected it with becomes right again

This entry is the same thing `/practice` does. That page marks five things the
CV claims and this site has not proven. This one does it **to a direction
rather than to a past.**

An unproven box is not the embarrassing part. Not writing down that it is
unproven is.
