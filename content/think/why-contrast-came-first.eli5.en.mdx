Usually you pick a colour first and check later whether it is easy to see. I reversed the order.

## What to know first

For text to be readable, **the text colour and the background colour have to be different enough.**
That difference, measured as a number, is called **contrast**, written as a ratio like `3.05 to 1`.
A bigger number means easier to see.

- Small body text needs **4.5 or more**.
- Large text only needs **3 or more**.

## What happened

I set the orange `#FF4D00` as the main colour.
Measured on the light paper background, it came out at **2.92**.
That fails even the large-text bar, never mind body text.

There were two options.

1. Make the orange darker — the colour I chose changes
2. Make the background brighter — the orange stays

I raised the background slightly. **3.05.** Pass.
**I moved the floor to keep the colour.**

## Then something interesting happened

The same orange can do different things depending on the background.

- **On a light background it is 3.05** — it cannot be text, only **a painted block.**
- **On a dark background it is 5.91** — it **can be text.**

So on the light side the orange shows up as a rectangular block,
and on the dark side it shows up as letters.

This is not sneaking around the rule. The rule created a rhythm.

> A good constraint does not reduce your options. It makes the ones you have left clearer.

## The rules left over

- Text placed on an orange block is **black on both the light and the dark side.**
  Paper-coloured text is 2.92 and does not pass.
- The outline that appears when you move around with the keyboard passes on both sides with one colour.

A checker recalculates these numbers every time the site is built.
If a number in the documentation disagrees with the real one, the build stops.
