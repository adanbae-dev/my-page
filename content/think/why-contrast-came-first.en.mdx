Art direction usually picks a palette first and checks accessibility later.
That order got reversed.

With `#FF4D00` set as the accent, measuring it on the paper ground gave **2.92:1**.
Not body text, and not even the 3:1 large-text threshold.

There were two options.

1. Darken the accent — the chosen colour changes
2. Lighten the ground — the accent stays

The ground went from `#F2F0EB` to `#F7F5F0`. 3.05:1. Pass.
**The floor moved so the colour could stay.**

## Where the constraint became the direction

Something interesting happens here. The accent is 3.05:1 on the light ground, so it can
only be used as a *field*; on the dark ground it is 5.91:1, so it can be *type*.

Same orange, different jobs depending on the ground.
On paper it can only be **painted**; on ink it can also **become a letter**.

So the same colour appears as a slab on the light side and as a letterform on the dark side.
This is not a way around the contrast constraint; the constraint produced the inversion rhythm.

> A good constraint does not reduce the options. It makes the remaining ones clearer.

## The rules that came out of it

- Type on an accent field is **ink in both tones**. The paper colour fails at 2.92:1.
- The focus ring is one value, `#CC3E00` — 4.53:1 on paper, 3.99:1 on ink. The inversion does not touch it.

`pnpm check:contrast` recalculates these numbers on every run.
If a value written in the docs disagrees with the real one, the build breaks.
